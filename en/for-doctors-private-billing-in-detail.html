Advancing private billing: what factoring really changes in a medical practice

Back to Factoring for Doctors

Statutory health insurance fees arrive quarterly, reliably, with interim payments. Late, but predictable. Private billing is the opposite: different amounts, different payment behaviour, different reimbursement times per insurer and allowance office. This is precisely the most profitable part of your revenue, and the hardest to plan. Factoring reverses that: your incoming payments follow the date you provided the service. What you treated in January is in your account in January.

What practices use the liquidity for

  • Equipment investment without leasing. Ultrasound, endoscopy, laser diagnostics from your own liquidity instead of multi-year leasing commitments.
  • Staff. An additional medical assistant costs full salary from day one and only pays for herself over months. Someone has to finance that lead time.
  • Practice takeover and succession. The purchase price is financed, working capital for the first few quarters often isn't.
  • Medical-centre and cooperation structures. Multiple locations multiply the working-capital need, not the bank's willingness to lend.
  • Elective and self-pay services. A growing segment with direct consumer receivables, and correspondingly higher default risk.

What happens with your patients, and what doesn't

AUREVIA deals with your patients in a factual and restrained manner. You determine how payment delays are handled, what payment arrangements are possible, and when matters are escalated. In sensitive individual cases, receivables can be excluded from the standard process at your request.

What doesn't happen: aggressive debt collection that damages your relationship with the patient. A lost patient costs you more over the years than one unpaid invoice.

Distinction from a private billing agency

A conventional billing agency issues and sends your invoices and pays out once the patient has paid or after an agreed period. Under non-recourse factoring, the receivable itself is purchased: you receive the purchase price independent of when payment arrives, and the default risk sits with the factor within the agreed scope. When comparing providers, check three things: whether credit protection is promised and to what extent, whether the model requires you to hand over your entire receivables portfolio, and whether the fee and the interest component are shown separately.

Consent in everyday practice

Assigning a doctor's fee receivable requires the patient's express, prior and written consent (Section 203 of the German Criminal Code, professional codes, settled case law of the Federal Court of Justice). In practice, that means the consent text belongs on the registration form and is signed at first contact, with a one-time catch-up for existing patients. Anyone who does not consent continues to be billed by you directly. If consent is treated as the exception rather than the standard, the proportion of purchasable receivables stays permanently low, this is the most common reason factoring projects in practices fall short of expectations.

Do I need to switch my practice software?

No. Billing data can be submitted as a file, via batch upload, or through an interface from your existing practice management software.

What about invoices that have already been outstanding for a while?

Whether and under what conditions already overdue receivables can be purchased depends on the agreed model and is clarified in the initial consultation.
