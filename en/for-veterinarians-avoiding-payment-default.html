In depth · Veterinarians

You bear one hundred percent of the default risk on your invoices yourself

Back to Factoring for Veterinarians

There is no payer standing behind your invoice. No statutory health insurance system, no allowance office, and in many cases no pet health insurance either. Your debtor is the animal owner directly, a private individual whose ability to pay you cannot check in the emergency room at two in the morning. Under true factoring, this risk transfers within the agreed scope.

Why the GOT reform has made the problem bigger

With the revision of the GOT (German veterinary fee schedule), invoice amounts have risen significantly. From a business perspective, this was necessary. The side effect: an invoice that used to be in the low three-figure range is now often above it, and lands with animal owners who did not expect an amount of this size. The arithmetic behind this is simple and still underestimated. If the average invoice amount rises and the default rate stays the same, your absolute loss rises proportionally. If the default rate rises along with the invoice amount, which experience suggests it does, the loss rises disproportionately.

Where it hurts most

  • Emergency/out-of-hours service. At night, on weekends, with an unfamiliar owner, high amounts, high emotional tension, and the worst starting point for a payment arrangement.
  • Surgery and inpatient treatment. Cruciate ligament repair, foreign-body surgery, intensive care: four-figure amounts owed by a consumer debtor.
  • Equine practice. High individual amounts, long travel distances, often several parties involved per horse, boarding stable, owner, share-rider.
  • Livestock practice. Herd health management with commercial debtors, long payment terms and a marked concentration on a small number of farms.
  • Expensive diagnostics. CT, MRI, lab work: you commission third-party services and pay for them before the owner pays.

What changes in your day-to-day treatment work

The most common question in veterinary practices is not about the fee, but about the treatment decision: how do you handle an owner when you suspect the invoice will not be paid? This decision becomes easier when the default risk, within the agreed scope, does not rest with you. Second point: the conversation about money does not belong in the treatment room. When payment monitoring, reminders and collection are handled by AUREVIA, your team no longer has to have that conversation, and your relationship with the owner remains a professional one.

Do I need the animal owner's consent?

The duty of confidentiality under Section 203 of the German Criminal Code applies to human medicine. For the assignment of veterinary fee receivables, a clear notice is nevertheless required under data protection law and by contract; we provide the wording for your treatment and admission documents.

Does this also apply to clinics and group practices?

Yes. With higher volumes and multiple locations, the capital tied up weighs more heavily, so the effect is correspondingly greater.

Put a number on your defaults.

Annual invoice volume, average invoice amount, write-offs on receivables from the past year: from this we build the comparison.
