For Veterinarians

Factoring for veterinarians: financial flexibility for practice and clinic.

At two in the morning, a dog comes in with gastric torsion. You operate, and whether the owner pays the invoice you find out four weeks later. Unlike in human medicine, there is no payer standing behind your invoice: no statutory health insurance system, no allowance office, and often no pet health insurance either. Your debtor is the animal owner directly. AUREVIA purchases your receivables and, under true factoring, can assume the agreed default risk.

Starting point

Typical liquidity situations

Since the GOT reform (German veterinary fee schedule), invoice amounts have risen significantly. From a business perspective this was necessary; the side effect is that a payment default now costs more in absolute terms than before.

Emergency/out-of-hours service is the worst starting point for a payment arrangement: at night, with an unfamiliar owner, a high amount, and high emotional tension.

Surgery and inpatient treatment generate four-figure amounts owed by a consumer debtor.

Equine practice: high individual amounts, long distances, often several parties involved per horse, owner, boarding stable, share-rider.

Expensive diagnostics are an upfront cost. You commission and pay for CT, MRI and lab work before the owner pays.

Livestock practice: commercial debtors with long payment terms and a marked concentration on a small number of farms.

Impact

What AUREVIA changes for you

  • Receivables turn into available working capital sooner.
  • Investments and purchasing can be timed more effectively.
  • Factoring supplements short-term liquidity but does not replace investment financing for construction projects or major equipment.
  • Under true factoring, the agreed default risk can transfer to AUREVIA, typically the stronger argument for veterinary practices than the timing advantage alone.
  • The conversation about money leaves the treatment room: payment monitoring and reminders for submitted receivables are handled by AUREVIA.
Process

How it works in a veterinary practice or clinic

Cash transactions remain unaffected; factoring concerns the receivables you invoice. After onboarding, you submit these digitally, supplemented by the assignment notice on the invoice. For consumer debtors, purchase takes place without prior individual review within the agreed amount limits; for commercial debtors in livestock practice and for pet health insurers, a limit review is carried out. Instalment payments can be accommodated within the agreed framework; for owners who cannot cover a large invoice in one sum, this is often the difference between treatment and forgoing it.

Legal framework

What is legally required

The duty of confidentiality under Section 203 of the German Criminal Code applies to human medicine and does not apply in the same way to veterinary fee receivables. Under data protection law and by contract, however, a clear notice to the animal owner is still required, since the owner's personal data is processed and transmitted to AUREVIA. We provide the corresponding wording for your treatment and admission documents.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions from veterinarians

What about cash-paying clients?

Cash transactions remain unaffected. Factoring concerns only receivables that you invoice.

Can I offer instalment payments?

Yes, within the contractually agreed framework. The specifics are defined during onboarding.

What about pet health insurers?

Receivables against insurers are commercial debtors with their own creditworthiness and are reviewed accordingly.
