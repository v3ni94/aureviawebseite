For Doctors

Factoring for doctors: liquidity when your work has long been done.

The patient has been treated, the invoice under the GOÄ fee schedule has gone out, and then, initially, nothing happens. They wait for reimbursement from their private health insurer or their allowance office, and only then do they transfer the payment to you. Weeks can pass between treatment and payment. Staff, rent, medical equipment and ongoing practice costs don't wait that long. With AUREVIA, you sell your receivables and make liquidity from work you have already performed available sooner.

Typical situations

Typical liquidity pressures

Private billing is your most profitable revenue segment, and also the hardest to plan. Different amounts, different payment behaviour, different reimbursement times per insurer.

Equipment and digitalisation cost money all at once. Ultrasound, endoscopy, practice IT: high capital needs against a trickle of incoming small amounts.

A new medical assistant costs full salary from day one and only pays for herself over months. The practice has to fund that lead time out of its own pocket.

Practice takeovers, growth, medical-centre structures: the purchase price is usually financed, working capital for the first few quarters rarely is.

Self-pay and elective services (IGeL) are growing, and with them the share of receivables owed directly by consumers.

Impact

What AUREVIA changes for you

  • Your incoming payments follow the date you provided the service, not the payment behaviour of others.
  • Liquidity is based on your receivables, not solely on an additional bank line.
  • Within the agreed model, you decide which receivables are submitted.
  • A digital process reduces the administrative burden at reception and in billing.
  • With non-recourse factoring, the agreed default risk on the receivable transfers to AUREVIA.
Process

How it works in a medical practice

After the one-time onboarding, nothing changes in how you bill: you keep invoicing under the GOÄ fee schedule, in your usual software. The only addition is the assignment notice with payment instructions, typically a one-time text block added to most practice management systems. Invoice data is submitted to AUREVIA individually or in batches; we check the purchase conditions and show you the terms. The purchase only takes place once you accept and the contractual conditions are met. No change of practice software is required.

Legal framework

What is legally required

Assigning a doctor's fee receivable to a third party is only permissible with the patient's express, prior and written consent. This follows from the physician's duty of confidentiality under Section 203 of the German Criminal Code, the professional codes of the regional medical associations, and the settled case law of the Federal Court of Justice. Without valid consent, the assignment is void and the receivable cannot be purchased.

This is not a formality; it is the precondition for factoring to work in your practice at all. AUREVIA provides you with vetted consent wording for your registration form and treatment agreement, so consent is obtained in daily practice without extra effort. Patients who do not consent continue to be billed by you directly.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions from doctors

Can I submit only part of my private billing?

Yes, under the agreed partial-portfolio model. You can, for example, submit only invoices above a certain amount or only certain service categories. Receivables that are not submitted are correspondingly not covered by credit protection.

What happens if a patient disputes the invoice on its merits?

Substantive objections to the billing remain your responsibility. Credit protection covers the debtor's insolvency and unwillingness to pay, not the validity of the claim itself. Where an objection is justified, the receivable is corrected or reassigned back under the contractual rules.

Does this also apply to fees from the statutory associations of panel doctors (KV)?

Receivables against associations of statutory health insurance physicians (KV) are subject to special restrictions on assignment. The focus here is on private billing, elective services and self-pay services; we review your specific situation individually.
