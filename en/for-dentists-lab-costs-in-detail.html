The lab-cost lever: why growth in a dental practice costs liquidity first

Back to Factoring for Dentists

With an implant restoration or extensive prosthetics, the outside-lab share accounts, depending on the case, for a substantial part of the invoice amount. You advance this share without any value creation of your own standing against it. The more high-value prosthetics you do, the higher your tied-up capital. Put differently: growth initially worsens your liquidity instead of improving it. With factoring, your practice no longer finances the material input between production and patient payment. Whether this pays off comes down to a single figure, your average outside-lab share. If it is low, the effect is minor. If it is high, the effect is substantial.

Instalment payment as a consultation argument

High-value restorations rarely fail on indication and often fail on price. If you can offer instalment payment without waiting months for your own fee and without carrying the default risk, that changes your consultations. AUREVIA can structure payment arrangements within the contractually agreed conditions.

Further typical use cases

  • Treatment and cost plans with a long lead time. Months often pass between drawing up the plan, approval, treatment and payment.
  • Practice equipment. CBCT, intraoral scanners, CAD/CAM units, investments that lower the outside-lab share in the long run but must be advanced first.
  • Building an in-house lab. Increases margin and capital commitment at the same time.
  • Practice takeovers. The goodwill is financed, the ongoing working capital often is not.
  • Orthodontics. Long treatment periods with an instalment structure and consumer debtors.

What credit protection covers, and what it does not

Under non-recourse factoring, the default protection covers the debtor's insolvency and unwillingness to pay. Rework, warranty and defect-liability rights remain a matter for the treatment agreement. Where a receivable is reduced for these reasons, it is corrected or reassigned back under the contractual rules. This distinction is not a technicality: in a claim, it decides who pays.

Can I exclude individual cases?

Yes, under the agreed partial-scope model, for example only prosthetics above a threshold amount.

How is the patient's share handled for statutory-insurance patients?

The co-payment share is a receivable against the patient and is therefore generally eligible for purchase.
