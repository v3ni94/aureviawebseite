Small structure, large upfront outlay: the professions in detail

Back to Factoring for Other Healthcare Professions

Not every profession in the healthcare sector has an established billing industry behind it. Smaller and specialised professions in particular carry the same pre-financing burden as large practices, only with less negotiating power and a thinner capital base. This page goes through the individual groups.

Three patterns common to all these professions

  1. Personnel costs fall due monthly, revenue does not. The higher your personnel share, the harder every delay in incoming payments hits you.
  2. The self-pay share is growing, and with it the default risk. A receivable against a private individual is a different matter from a receivable against a cost carrier.
  3. Banks are cautious in assessing small healthcare businesses. Little collateral, often a freelance structure, limited balance-sheet history. Factoring, by contrast, is assessed on the quality of your receivables.

The professions and their particularities

  • Alternative and complementary medicine practitioners (Heilpraktiker). Almost entirely self-pay and supplementary-insurance receivables, wide dispersion, no cost-carrier backing. Credit protection (del credere) is usually the decisive argument here. Subject to the duty of confidentiality under Section 203 of the German Criminal Code; consent required.
  • Speech therapy. Allied-health billing with a long payment lead time, a high personnel-cost ratio, and the same clawback risk as in physiotherapy.
  • Occupational therapy. Comparable structure, with additional equipment and material requirements.
  • Podiatry / chiropody. A mix of prescribed services and a high self-pay share, often numerous small-value receivables.
  • Midwives and birth centres. Billing across multiple cost carriers, professional liability insurance as a substantial fixed-cost item due upfront. Duty of confidentiality applies; consent required.
  • Psychotherapy practices. Private billing over long treatment periods, particularly sensitive data. The strictest standard applies here: keep the scope of transmitted information as small as possible.
  • Home care services. Monthly personnel costs against delayed billing with cost carriers, high pre-financing need, often a thin equity base.
  • Medical supply stores, orthopaedic technology, orthopaedic shoe technology. Material pre-financing, cost-carrier approval requirements, long billing routes. Deliveries to facilities are purely commercial receivables with no consent requirement.
  • Hearing care / audiology retail and optometry with a supply component. A mix of retail and statutory health insurance billing; the supply component ties up capital, the retail component does not.
  • Nutrition counselling, osteopathy, medical foot care. Predominantly private invoicing, often without any dedicated receivables management.

What needs separate clarification for your profession

For all professions subject to the duty of confidentiality under Section 203 of the German Criminal Code, assigning a fee receivable requires the express, prior and written consent of the person treated. This restriction does not apply to purely commercial receivables, for example a medical supply store's deliveries to a care facility. Which of your receivables fall into which category is clarified in the initial consultation.
