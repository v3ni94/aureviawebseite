Factoring & Financing

Financing from your receivables, instead of thinking about liquidity only in terms of credit.

Factoring is the ongoing purchase of receivables under a framework agreement. You receive liquidity from work you have already performed before your debtor pays in the normal course. Regulatorily, factoring is classified as a financial service under Section 1(1a) sentence 2 no. 9 KWG, regardless of whether the default risk is assumed.

Model 1

Non-recourse factoring: financing plus agreed default protection

With non-recourse factoring, the factor assumes, to the contractually defined extent, the default risk of the purchased, undisputed receivable. For you, this means you not only receive liquidity sooner, but also reduce the economic risk that a debtor fails to pay a properly existing receivable for creditworthiness reasons. Risks relating to the claim's veracity, warranty, objections and other contractually excluded risks remain unaffected.

Credit protection (del credere) refers specifically to this assumption of credit risk, not the assumption of every conceivable reason an invoice might go unpaid.

Model 2

Recourse factoring: financing with recourse

With recourse factoring, the financing function is in the foreground. The default risk generally remains with the factoring client, or a contractual right of recourse applies. This can make economic sense where the focus is on fast liquidity, or where receivables or debtors do not fit a credit-protection model.

Important for classification purposes: economically, recourse factoring behaves like a secured credit line. The advance amount tends to increase the balance sheet total rather than shorten it; the relieving effects on the equity ratio and rating do not arise in the same way as with non-recourse factoring.

Which model does AUREVIA offer?

AUREVIA primarily offers non-recourse factoring, to the extent the type of receivable, debtor, risk and contractual requirements allow. For suitable constellations, recourse factoring may alternatively be offered. The specific product, the scope of credit protection and any rights of recourse are clearly stated in the offer and in the factoring agreement.

A bank essentially finances your past: balance-sheet history, collateral, personal creditworthiness. Factoring ties to a service already rendered. For self-employed professionals and smaller healthcare businesses, that is a structural difference, because tangible collateral is often unavailable or already pledged.

Factoring does not replace a bank in every situation. Long-term investments, real estate, large medical equipment fit better with classic investment financing. AUREVIA therefore positions factoring as a specialised working-capital building block.

And frankly: measured purely on interest, factoring is not the cheapest way to raise money. But it includes services a loan does not: default protection with non-recourse factoring, debtor management, ongoing credit monitoring. The meaningful comparison is therefore not fee against interest, but total cost against total benefit, including avoided defaults, saved working time and achievable purchasing advantages.

Frankly speaking

When factoring is not right for you

For the decision to be sound, this belongs here too: factoring generally does not pay off if your customers mostly pay immediately and in cash, if your receivables volume is permanently below EUR 60,000 per year, or if your problem is not a liquidity gap but a structural earnings weakness. In the latter case, factoring shifts a problem rather than solving it, and we will tell you so in the initial conversation.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is factoring the same as a loan?

No. Factoring is based on the purchase of receivables. With a classic loan, you receive borrowed capital that must be repaid.

What is the key difference between non-recourse and recourse factoring?

With non-recourse factoring, the factor assumes the agreed default risk; with recourse factoring, this risk generally remains with the client, or a right of recourse applies.

Does credit protection also cover legitimate objections?

No. If a receivable is not substantively valid, due to a defect, incorrect billing or a service not rendered, that falls within the client's area of responsibility. Credit protection covers credit risks, not performance disputes.

What is the security retention?

A withheld portion of the invoice amount that covers deductions, credit notes and cash discounts. It is paid out once payment is received or the period expires, and is not a cost component.

Do I have to sell all my receivables?

That depends on the agreed factoring model. Under a selective model, receivables are submitted according to clear rules; there is no default protection for receivables not submitted.

What does factoring cost?

Costs depend on the type of receivable, volume, term, debtor structure, risk and scope of services. The relevant terms are shown transparently before purchase.

Can I combine factoring and bank financing?

In principle yes, provided contracts, collateral and receivables assignments are compatible with one another. Existing blanket assignments or security rights must be reviewed in advance.

Does factoring require a licence?

Yes. Factoring is a financial service under Section 1(1a) sentence 2 no. 9 KWG and is subject to supervision. Status details can be found in the Imprint.
